SCHOOL LIFE

School Uniform

The John Paul College uniform identifies students as part of the school community and is expected to be worn correctly at all times.

  • Only approved items on the school uniform list should be worn.
  • Years 7–10 and Years 11–13 wear different uniforms.
  • Summer and winter uniforms are different.
  • Items should be clearly named, clean, ironed and repaired.
  • Full uniform should be worn while travelling between school and home.

Mrs Jo de Loryn will help you organise your uniform during orientation. The School Uniform Shop is open Monday and Wednesday from 8:30am to 2:00pm.

ACADEMIC PLACEMENT

Subject Placement

New students complete the Oxford Placement Test as early as possible. It is used to help staff understand which courses may be most suitable, rather than as a pass-or-fail test.

Placement is decided with the International Dean based on the student's needs, English language competence, previous school records and available spaces in classes.

JPC will try to place international students into their preferred subjects, but this cannot always be guaranteed, particularly when students arrive part-way through the year and some classes are already full.

Helpful tip

Submitting subject choices before the academic year begins makes preferred placements easier to arrange. If a combination does not work, changes can still be discussed.

ACADEMIC PROGRESS

Assessments & Reports

Students are expected to attempt the educational assessments in the classes they join. Exemptions are only made after discussions involving the student, teacher and, where appropriate, agents and/or parents.

Students may attempt subject assessments without necessarily enrolling with NZQA.

Reports are issued twice a year.
Special reports can also be arranged when a student's home country has specific reporting requirements.

SETTLING IN

Culture Shock

Moving to another country can feel exciting at first, but adapting to a new environment can also bring worry, homesickness or feeling down. These reactions are common for many new students.

With support, time and a positive approach, students normally become more comfortable and integration becomes easier. Some students may experience similar feelings again when returning home.
